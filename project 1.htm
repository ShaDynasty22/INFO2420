Web Site Critiques

Website Evaluation Questions

  1. What was your first impression of this site?
  2. How does this site organize the flow of information?
  3. Does the site structure make sense or does it confuse you? Why?
  4. Does the site's graphics, layout, and multimedia enhance the site or detract from it? Why?
  5. How long did it take for the home page to load? Was the load time reasonable?
  6. Was the text color and size readable?
  7. Did the site have a navigation bar or other navigation tool?
  8. Did the site have a consistent color scheme and look?
  9. How does the site respond to smaller window sizes?
  10. What does this site offer that was unusual or very well done?

Trader Joe's - https://www.traderjoes.com/

Website: https://www.traderjoes.com/

1. First Impression

Part of me regrets picking Trader Joe's, simply because I am a Trader Joe's hater. I always have been, and always will be. If Trader Joe's has no haters, I am dead.
Despite my feelings towards Trader Joe's, their website is not as bad as their stores. It's not great, but I have seen worse.
The landing page is very bright, and opening it with my screen brightness turned up felt like getting flashbanged. It is off-white though, so it wasn't as blinding.
The homepage has an easy to access menu, with many links to items customers may want. A search button is easily found. Plenty of information is available immediately.

2. Organization and Information Flow

The menu button isn't clearly labeled with the word "menu", but everyone who has gone to a website in their life recognizes the hamburger style menu icon.
It is persistent once clicked on, so when I click on it, the menu stays visible. I do have to click an "x" if I want to close it though. Not great.
Immediately after opening the website, the homepage gives the shopper lots of information. Recipes, podcasts, products, and announcements are all presented without the user having to go looking for them.
I didn't click open this page because I wanted to know about their new lemon pepper, but the page presented it to me and now I am interested. So I guess their strategy worked.

3. Site Structure

The site structure mostly makes sense, especially for a company that wants to sell me products.
The homepage immediately directs me toward products, recipes, announcements, and other content, while the menu gives me a way to look for something more specific.
I never really felt confused about where I was supposed to go. If I wanted products, I could find products. If I wanted recipes, I could find recipes. If I wanted to find a store, that was also easy enough to find.
The structure isn't doing anything groundbreaking, but I don't really need Trader Joe's to reinvent website navigation. It works.

4. Graphics, Layout, and Multimedia

Trader Joe's definitely has a very specific look, and their website commits to it. The graphics look like they belong to the same company as the weird little signs and product labels in their stores.
There are a lot of product photos and illustrations, which makes sense because the entire point is to make food look good enough that I will go buy it. Unfortunately for me, this does occasionally work.
The layout is fairly clean even though there is a lot happening on the homepage. The different sections are separated enough that I don't feel like everything is fighting for my attention at the same time.
Overall, I think the graphics enhance the website. I may not particularly enjoy the Trader Joe's aesthetic, but it is recognizable, consistent, and fits the company.

5. Loading Time

The homepage loaded almost immediately for me. I didn't have to sit there staring at a blank screen or wait for the images to slowly appear one at a time.
Considering how many pictures and different sections are on the homepage, I expected it to take longer than it did.
The load time was completely reasonable and wasn't something I would have even noticed if this assignment hadn't specifically asked me to pay attention to it.

6. Text and Background

The text is easy to read for the most part. The font sizes are large enough, and there is enough contrast between the text and the background that I don't have to work to read anything.
As I mentioned before, the background is extremely bright. It isn't quite pure white, which probably saves it from being genuinely painful, but there is still a lot of very light space on the page.
They also use darker colors and images throughout the site, which helps break up all that brightness.
I don't think the background hurts the usability of the site, but I personally would not complain if somebody turned the brightness down a little.

7. Navigation

The website does have navigation tools, primarily the hamburger menu at the top of the page along with search and other links.
The navigation is pretty easy to understand once the menu is open. The categories are labeled clearly enough that I can tell where each link is going to take me before I click it.
I still don't love having to manually close the menu with the "x" every time I am done with it. That isn't a huge problem, but it is one of those tiny things that annoys me more every time I have to do it.
Other than that, the navigation works well. I didn't get lost, which is probably the most important thing a navigation menu can accomplish.

8. Consistent Design

Trader Joe's is extremely consistent with its design. I clicked through several different pages and they all very obviously belonged to the same website.
The same general colors, fonts, illustrations, navigation tools, and overall style appear throughout the site.
Even when the content changes from products to recipes or other information, the site doesn't suddenly look like I have been sent somewhere else.
Whatever complaints I have about the actual look of Trader Joe's, I can't really complain that they don't know what their brand is. They absolutely do.

9. Responsive Design

When I made the browser window smaller, the website adjusted instead of just cutting off half of the page.
The images and sections became smaller or moved around to fit the available space, and the text was still readable. I didn't have to scroll sideways to see information that disappeared off the edge of the screen.
The hamburger menu also makes more sense when the window is smaller because it keeps the navigation from taking up a huge amount of space.
Overall, the responsive design works well. The site is still usable at a smaller size and doesn't look like somebody designed it exclusively for one specific computer monitor in 2007.

10. Unique or Well-Designed Features

The thing Trader Joe's does particularly well is making the website feel like Trader Joe's. There are thousands of grocery store websites, and most of them are not exactly memorable. This one is.
The combination of illustrations, product photography, recipes, announcements, and other content gives people reasons to browse instead of only visiting when they need a store address.
I also think featuring new and unusual products directly on the homepage is smart. I went to the website specifically to criticize it for an assignment and somehow still ended up learning about products I might actually try.
I remain a Trader Joe's hater, but I will reluctantly admit that their website does its job pretty well.
